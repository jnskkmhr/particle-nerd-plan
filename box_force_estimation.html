G1-sized testbed: 0.02 m grid and synchronized GRF

The new reference uses a 2 × 2 × 0.3 m sand bed, matching the G1 tilting testbed's dimensions, with a level fixed tray and a freely moving 2 kg box. The MPM grid is 0.02 m, with 296,875 material points, approximately 0.016 m particle spacing, and 25 solver iterations. Every particle is simulated and rendered.

GRF means the vertical force exerted by sand on the box, positive upward. The force profile is aligned with the box motion, including the coupler's one-step delay. Both views use recorded MPM particles; the estimated box cannot change the sand motion. No MPM solver or force network runs in the estimated-box replay.

Earlier experiments, reference validation, and limitations

Correction to data collection

The original collector prescribed the box trajectory. This was inconsistent with the intended experiment. The revised collector gives a 2 kg box a random initial position, orientation, and velocity, then advances it with MJWarp coupled to MPM sand. It does not command the box after reset.

For a freely moving box, the measured change in linear momentum should equal gravity plus all applied contact impulses. With lagged coupling, the MPM reaction computed in one step is applied in the next rigid-body step. Both quantities are recorded so the comparison uses matching intervals. Any direct rigid-ground or rigid-wall contacts must also be included.

Dynamic reference validation

Four 400-step episodes completed with 500 material particles per episode. The mean box force-balance residual is 0.000002 N. The ground and walls remained fixed. The sand reaction at the end of the episodes was 19.3–20.6 N upward, close to the 2 kg box’s weight of 19.62 N. No direct rigid contact occurred.

This checks the reference simulation, not force prediction. Force estimation is evaluated separately below.

Dynamic force estimation: first improvement

On 160 identical frames, the new body-contact approximation reduced box-force MSE by 44.7%, from 59.15 to 32.71 N². Force-vector RMSE decreased from 13.32 to 9.91 N. It uses box mass, inertia, and contact velocity conditions; it does not use measured future box acceleration.

On eight separate dense test episodes, force-vector RMSE was 10.69 N. Some impact peaks remain inaccurate, and steady support was 12–15 N rather than the reference 19.62 N. Adding Coulomb friction improved torque error but increased force error to 14.46 N.

Scope: this method estimates the dynamic box reaction using reference particle motion. It has not been validated with learned particle motion or in a full coupled rollout. The former method estimates reactions on all six bodies, so runtime comparisons have different scopes.

Can the estimated forces reproduce box motion?

The new replay integrates the box from its initial state using estimated contact forces, gravity, and the recorded coupling delay. Its position is not reset to the reference trajectory. Particle trajectories remain recorded, so this is not yet a fully coupled learned simulation.

Interpolating material velocity at the box surface and adding a small penetration correction reduced mean episode position RMSE from 2.29 to 1.13 cm. Mean orientation RMSE changed from 9.57 to 8.93°. Force-vector RMSE decreased from 10.69 to 9.85 N on the same 3,200 evaluation transitions. Reference-force replay reproduced box position within 1.34 micrometers RMSE on average, checking force timing and integration.

The early frictional variant caused the replayed box to intersect rigid ground in all eight episodes. Those trajectories are invalid after intersection because this diagnostic does not resolve direct rigid contact. All variants still need validation with predicted particle motion. These evaluation episodes have been reused for development; a fresh final test is planned after fixing the method.

Independent evaluation and motion video

With fixed parameters, the corrected frictional model gives 9.41 N force-vector RMSE on 32 newly collected episodes (12,800 steps). Component force MSE is 29.50 N² and torque-vector RMSE is 0.344 N m. The episode-bootstrap 95% interval for force RMSE is 8.91–9.87 N.

Estimated-force replay averages 0.72 cm position RMSE and 4.35° orientation RMSE. The Newton RTX video shows synchronized 3D views of the episode with median position error: blue for the MPM reference box, orange for the box driven by estimated forces. Particle trajectories remain recorded; this is not a coupled NeRD rollout.

Heavier boxes and particle-velocity errors expose remaining limitations. Force error is still about 35% relative to the reference norm for the 2 kg box. A mechanics test also corrected a friction-direction error for anisotropic rotational inertia; the corrected and earlier independent-test force RMSE differ by less than 0.001 N.

What the complete particle equations establish

Fixing the estimated box contacts allows internal particle forces and fixed-body reactions to satisfy particle momentum balance closely. However, changing the upward box force from 0 to 13.15 to 26.31 N also admits small balance residuals, because ground-and-wall support changes from 256.74 to 269.89 to 283.04 N. Particle balance alone cannot select the correct box reaction. This numerical example illustrates the force ambiguity explained in the methodology deck.

Across 320 noiseless validation samples, 50 contact solver iterations matched the converged result. These solver iterations are separate from the MPM reference iteration count.
