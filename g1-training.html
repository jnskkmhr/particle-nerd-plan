G1 particle NeRD evaluation

Both new versions use an NN force model. Version 1 freezes the particle predictor and trains the NN body-impulse head. Version 2 co-trains the particle predictor and NN body-impulse head. Version 1 validation results are below. The new 20-second evaluations are in progress; existing videos still show the analytical-force baseline.

22 October 2026. Twenty seconds requested per evaluation. The analytical-baseline dynamic simulations below use effective material mass multiplier 50, 200 solver iterations and relaxation 0.02. Particle model: validation-selected update 900, trained with a 0.5-second recurrent horizon.

Native G1 MPM newton_rtx settings: 1920 × 1080 per simulation view, read directly from the task configuration with no resolution override. Task-authored materials, particle widths, sky lighting and robot-tracking camera are retained. Two views plus plots form a 3840 × 1400 video. Use the full-resolution links or fullscreen for inspection.

Evaluation geometry: 3 × 3 × 0.3 m sand, 88,360 particles, 0.04 m MPM grid. Physics/prediction: 200 Hz; policy: 50 Hz. These MPM reference recordings are evaluation-only. Both NN force-model versions reuse the existing G1 training dataset.

Version 1: frozen particle predictor + NN body-impulse head

Selected checkpoint: update 1000. The particle predictor is unchanged; only the shared per-foot NN body-impulse head was trained. Force and torque are computed from its predicted linear and angular impulses divided by the timestep; the analytical force estimator is not used. Evaluation uses 50 validation trajectory windows, each with 100 autoregressive steps at 200 Hz (0.5 seconds), conditioned on recorded robot motion.

Analytical baseline: full dynamic simulation

In these baseline videos, particle NeRD predicts particle motion and the analytical impulse solver estimates sand-on-foot forces and torques. These analytical estimates drive MJWarp robot dynamics; they are not outputs of the NN body-impulse head. The policy observes the simulated robot. GRF here means the vertical force exerted by sand on each robot foot.
