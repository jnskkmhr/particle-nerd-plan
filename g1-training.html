2. Model training

Transolver receives three particle-position frames, current collider geometry and velocity, and material features. It predicts particle displacement for the next 5 ms. Training uses two autoregressive prediction steps and a loss that separately averages moving and stationary particle errors, so stationary particles cannot dominate solely through their count. All particles are processed; no region selector is used in this experiment.

Robot collider motion comes from the reference recording during training. The training split updates model weights; the validation split selects the checkpoint. Test trajectories are reserved for final evaluation. Initial cloud training uses four GPUs with a shared dataset copy; the full dataset job is prepared for eight GPUs.

4. Completed local checks

  • The installed and training Transolver implementations give identical outputs on a recorded G1 input.
  • On nine sampled reference frames, displacement divided by 5 ms agrees with recorded MPM particle velocity to approximately 0.000022 m/s RMS. This conversion does not explain the large rollout error. Measurements.
  • Two simultaneous five-second reference simulations: 1,001 frames each, 5 ms spacing, no native termination in these two trials.
  • Seven collision boxes per environment: two robot feet and five native sand-boundary boxes.
  • Joint state and applied-generalized-force recording passed a separate short two-environment test.
  • Two training steps with two-step rollout and validation completed for 88,360 particles; peak PyTorch allocated memory approximately 2.18 GiB on an RTX 5090. This is a runtime check, not an accuracy result.
