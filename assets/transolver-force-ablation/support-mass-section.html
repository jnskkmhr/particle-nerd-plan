07 / Effective material-mass multiplier: fixed-state experiment

The mass multiplier explains much of the stationary support deficit in these tests. Increasing ×10 to ×300 raises pre-drag support from 37.6 N to 71.6 N; the reference is approximately 73.6 N. Results are almost unchanged when particle and box velocities are explicitly zeroed. This supports the effective-mass response as a cause, rather than particle-velocity prediction error alone.

The 7.5 kg box requires 73.575 N upward support. The first two force columns retain recorded MPM particle endpoint velocities and box velocities. The final column sets particle velocities and box linear/angular velocities to zero, retains the same geometry, and computes box velocity from gravity plus the estimated force over 20 ms. Negative vz means downward motion.

Protocol: 12 reference snapshots before circle dragging (0–0.22 s), plus eight after the drop has settled (2.0–4.8 s at 0.4 s spacing). Each snapshot is held fixed across six multipliers and two velocity modes: 240 solves. External forces are zero. Contact count stayed unchanged for each snapshot/mode across multipliers; every solve converged. Stabilization, friction, contact geometry, and timestep were unchanged. The auxiliary particle velocity correction exists inside the estimator equations; it does not update the stored MPM particles.

Both are virtual-inertia parameters, but they act on different sides of the contact and are not interchangeable. Newton’s generic Proxy mapping can also scale particle proxies, but the G1 configuration inspected here maps rigid feet into MPM. The current box reference configuration likewise maps rigid bodies into MPM and leaves proxy mass_scale at its default 1.0. This experiment changes neither the reference proxy multiplier nor the physical particle/box masses.

Interpretation: a larger effective material mass makes the approximate material response resist more downward motion, so the box receives more support. It does not establish that ×300 is a physically correct material parameter or a generally accurate choice. Geometry still matters and the estimated zero-velocity box does not remain exactly at rest.

Default unchanged: ×10. Impact, dragging, torque accuracy, and full closed-loop stability have not been evaluated at the larger multipliers. Those comparisons are needed before selecting a new default.
