Box and shovel: 0.5-second recurrent training

Updated 2026-10-21 21:21 UTC · OSMO pool isaac-lab-l40s-03.

Training horizon: 25 consecutive predictions × 0.02 s = 0.5 s. Each predicted particle state becomes the input to the next prediction. Loss is accumulated across all 25 predictions, with gradients through the complete sequence. There are no particle-state resets or detached recurrent states inside the sequence.

What is being repeated

The saved configurations confirm that the later box and shovel checkpoints already used 0.5-second recurrent training. The G1 checkpoint used only 10 ms. These new box and shovel runs continue their matching 0.5-second-trained checkpoints; they are not a comparison of short versus long training horizons.

Training sequence

  1. Initialize particle history from three recorded position frames.
  2. Use the current particle history and recorded rigid-body pose/velocity to predict particle displacement over 20 ms.
  3. Add displacement to the current positions and append the predicted positions to the history.
  4. Repeat steps 2–3 until 25 predictions cover 0.5 s.
  5. Compare every predicted position with the corresponding MPM position. Average errors separately for moving and stationary particle-time samples, then give each nonempty group equal weight. Backpropagate through the entire sequence.

The moving group uses reference displacement greater than 0.5 mm from the last initialization frame. The labels come from MPM, not the model. Collider geometry is queried again from predicted particles at every step; shovel features use its concave signed-distance field.

Evaluation protocol

Evaluate both the previous and newly selected checkpoints on the same complete validation and test splits. Report particle-position error after 0.5 s and after five seconds, stationary-particle drift, failures, and the fixed-particle baseline. Test data is not used for optimization or checkpoint selection. The reference and prediction use identical recorded box or shovel motion.

More recurrent training does not add missing material-state inputs or new scooping trajectories. Improvement must be established from the new measurements.
