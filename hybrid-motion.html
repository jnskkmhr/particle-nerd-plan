How to read the comparison

  • Both runs start from the same particle and body state. Launch cases use the initial world velocities written above each video. Positive rotation follows the right-hand rule about the stated world axis; z points upward.
  • The blue box uses 50 exact contact/material solver sweeps. The muted green box uses 2 exact sweeps, without learned refinement. A pale stripe on the box shows its orientation.
  • For dragging, both runs receive the same cursor path. The spring force depends on each run’s box motion, so their applied forces can differ. A pale cursor and tether mark the grab point and target.
  • The moving cursors in the graphs indicate simulation time. Graphs show the vertical sand force actually applied to the box, the rigid tray’s vertical reaction on the sand, and the magnitude of sand torque applied to the box.

Simulation and dragging conditions

One 1.5 kg box (0.12 × 0.12 × 0.08 m) interacts with 4,000 particles in a 0.32 × 0.32 × 0.16 m bed. The rigid ground and four retaining walls remain fixed. Initial particle jitter is up to 20% of spacing. Material density is 1,200 kg/m³, Young’s modulus 1 MPa, and friction 0.4. Each rollout lasts 3 s with a 5 ms timestep.

Dragging acts from 0.5 to 2.7 s at a box-local offset of (0.035, 0, 0.025) m from its center of mass. A seeded cursor changes target every 0.5 s using smooth interpolation. The applied force is 180 × (cursor − grab point) − 12 × grab-point velocity, with magnitude capped at 45 N. The resulting torque is grab offset × force. No box position or velocity is prescribed.

Sand-on-box graphs use the previous MPM step’s feedback actually applied by the lagged rigid-body coupling. Tray reaction is the negative of current sand-on-tray forces, summed over the rigid ground and four walls. Fifty sweeps are a numerical comparison, not certified ground truth. These motion cases are separate from the timing benchmark. Simulations ran on horde_5080; RTX replay rendering ran on the local RTX 5090.
