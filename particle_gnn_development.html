GNN design to test

Independent recording checks verify finite data, 50 Hz particle timestamps, 251 post-settlement states, the settling criterion, one-step force-feedback alignment, translational force balance, and reconstruction of every applied disturbance from its recorded parameters.

Predict particle position and velocity every 20 ms from motion history, neighboring particles, material parameters, rigid geometry and rigid-surface velocity. No reference stress or deformation state is required at inference. Keep grid spacing and particle sampling fixed for this first experiment; approximately 0.07 m is the proposed graph radius at this sampling.

Compare the existing model with a model that limits the intruder’s direct effect to possible contact and propagates disturbance through particle neighbors. Continue to model gravity, settling and relaxation after contact ends. A blanket rule that freezes every particle not touching the box would suppress valid motion. Include undisturbed controls and measure accumulated drift. All particles remain represented; no region selector is required.

Evaluate 50 Hz learned motion both directly and after interpolation to the 200 Hz rigid-body/force-estimation interval. Interpolation cannot recover unresolved impact peaks. Retain a small high-frequency validation subset to quantify lost contact information. No interpolation accuracy has been established yet.

Dataset, OSMO training, and local inference

  1. Review these examples. Check the terrain, box size, settling, disturbance strength, and force profiles before committing to a collection campaign.
  2. Collect 1,000 five-second trajectories. Use independent seeds and randomized disturbances after settling. Reserve full episodes for validation and testing. Start with sand; material diversity needs additional parameter coverage and inputs.
  3. Store bounded-size files in object storage. Float32 particle position plus velocity requires 98.7 GB for 1,000 × 251 states, before compression. Constants are stored once per episode. Settling recordings and a high-frequency validation subset add storage.
  4. Run collection and training through OSMO. Reuse the IsaacLab code-snapshot and output/checkpoint workflow. Measure environments per GPU before selecting batch size; 100 simultaneous environments is not yet verified. Stream recording to disk instead of retaining complete trajectories on GPU. GNN distributed training needs its own data loader and gradient synchronization.
  5. Download the selected checkpoint for local inference. Evaluate the GNN with analytical body-force reconstruction and MJWarp, without MPM. Report particle drift, ground penetration, force error, box-motion error and full-step latency.

The previous selected GNN used only 48 training episodes of 1.5 s. The later 36-episode diversity pilot did not consistently improve coupled forces and box motion. These new previews are reference-data checks, not evidence of improved learned prediction. No large collection or training job will start before review.
