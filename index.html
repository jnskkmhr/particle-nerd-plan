Predict position and velocity separately

xᵢⁿ, vᵢⁿ: current particle position and velocity. A hat denotes the predicted next state. δvᵢpos: learned correction to displacement rate. δvᵢvel: learned endpoint-velocity increment. Both are in m/s; Δt = 0.02 s.

x̂ᵢⁿ⁺¹ = xᵢⁿ + Δt (vᵢⁿ + δvᵢpos)
v̂ᵢⁿ⁺¹ = vᵢⁿ + δvᵢvel

The decoder scales both outputs by 0.2 m/s. Endpoint velocity is predicted separately; it is not obtained by dividing predicted displacement by Δt.

What is learned

Gravity, material interaction, and boundary response are included in the learned corrections. There is no separate gravity integration or geometric projection in the current particle update.

The activity mask freezes inactive particles after this update. Endpoint velocity is supervised separately from displacement.
07 / Particle GNN: Region Selection Enabled by --selector

Predict which particles should move

Input: current features only

Particle velocity history and material parameters; local box/ground features; neighboring particle speeds. The selector is an MLP, not another GNN.

Apply the mask to the GNN output

Active: keep predicted next position and velocity.
Inactive: keep current position; set next velocity to zero.

Train the selector separately

A reference particle is active if it moves more than 0.5 mm from its current position within the next 0.1 s. Future MPM positions supply training labels only; inference uses current features.

Current limit: all particles still enter GNN message passing. The mask reduces unwanted motion; it does not yet reduce graph computation.

Results: region selection reduces stationary-particle drift. Those tests replay recorded box motion; they do not establish accurate coupled box support. ScoopBot instead predicts a spatial RoI and runs dynamics on the active subgraph.
48 input values: 26 particle-node values, 18 masked collider values, two collider influence values, and two neighboring-speed values. SiLU hidden layers. The threshold is stored in the selector checkpoint. No future particle or box state enters inference.

x̂ᵢ, v̂ᵢ: predicted next particle position and velocity. xᵢ,ref, vᵢ,ref: corresponding MPM targets. wᵢ: training weight. Δt: recording interval (0.02 s). L: weighted prediction loss.

L = Σᵢ wᵢ [‖(x̂ᵢ − xᵢ,ref) / Δt‖² + 0.5 ‖v̂ᵢ − vᵢ,ref‖²] / Σᵢ wᵢ

Every particle contributes. Higher weights emphasize particles near the box and particles moving in the reference simulation.

Each training sample starts from recorded state. Repeated prediction from the GNN’s own state is evaluated separately.

Velocity-history noise is added during training. AdamW updates the GNN; the analytical contact solver and MJWarp are outside this loss.
09 / Contact Force Estimation: Method

Contact forces for a box or an actuated robot

Notation: particles and rigid objects

vᵢⁿ, v̂ᵢⁿ⁺¹Particle i’s current velocity and GNN-predicted next velocity. Lowercase v always refers to material. Vgen, VCOMRobot base/joint velocity vector; rigid-body center-of-mass linear velocity. Uppercase V always refers to rigid objects. Vsurface,iWorld velocity of the rigid-body point at contact location sᵢ. It includes rigid-body translation and rotation. n, n+1, ΔtCurrent step, next step, and timestep. A hat denotes a prediction; * denotes free motion before material contact.
Input: particle motion prediction, masses, rigid-object state, geometry and known loads. Output: material force and torque on each rigid body.

The effective material mass κmᵢ is retained. Step 3 defines this assumption. Geometry stays fixed during the contact solve.

1. Include actuation in free motion

Known loads determine free motion before material contact.

τactGeneralized actuator force after actuator dynamics and transmission. τpassiveGeneralized force from passive elements, such as joint springs and viscous damping. τexternalKnown applied loads, such as a user’s push or drag, mapped to generalized coordinates. Excludes actuation, passive forces, gravity and the unknown material reaction.

q: current robot configuration. M(q): robot mass matrix. b(q, Vgenⁿ): gravity, Coriolis and centrifugal bias forces. These forces act on base and joint degrees of freedom.

Vgenⁿ: current robot velocity. Vgen*: free robot velocity after timestep Δt. τknown: sum of the three forces above.

τknown = τact + τpassive + τexternal
Vgen* = Vgenⁿ + Δt M(q)⁻¹ [τknown − b(q, Vgenⁿ)]
Explicit velocity step shown. If MJWarp already supplies the free velocity, use it directly; do not add these forces or gravity again.

2. Evaluate both velocities at the contact point

A. Material velocity evaluated at the rigid surface

xᵢⁿ; sᵢCurrent particle position; nearest point on the rigid-body surface. v̂ᵢⁿ⁺¹Next particle velocity predicted by the GNN from current state/history. This model predicts velocity separately from position. Gᵢ3 × 3 spatial gradient of predicted particle velocity, fitted from neighboring particles by weighted least squares. Units: 1/s. v̂surface,iPredicted material velocity evaluated at the surface point sᵢ.
v̂surface,i = v̂ᵢⁿ⁺¹ + Gᵢ(sᵢ − xᵢⁿ)

Gᵢ times the position offset adjusts particle velocity to the surface location. This is spatial extrapolation, not another time integration.

B. Rigid-body velocity at that same surface point

q; VgenCurrent robot configuration; generalized velocity vector containing base and joint velocities. Jᵢ(q)3 × Ndof point Jacobian. It maps the robot’s Ndof velocity components to world velocity at sᵢ. Vsurface,iVelocity of the rigid-body point located at sᵢ.
Vsurface,i = Jᵢ(q)Vgen

For a free box, VCOM is center-of-mass velocity, ω is angular velocity, c is COM position, and rᵢ = sᵢ − c is the contact arm.

Vsurface,i = VCOM + ω × rᵢ

Initially use free velocity Vgen*. Step 3 updates the temporary velocity during contact iterations.

C. Compare two different objects at one location

Vsurface,i: rigid-body point velocity.
v̂surface,i: material velocity extrapolated to the same point.

VCOMⁿ and vᵢⁿ are different states. The first belongs to the rigid body’s center of mass; the second belongs to particle i. Neither determines the other.

Contact impulses respond to their relative motion. Step 3 includes the effective material response before computing the final relative velocity.

All linear velocities here are in world coordinates, in m/s. A stationary, rotating box can have zero COM velocity and nonzero surface velocity.

3. Solve individual contact impulses

A. Initialize rigid-object and effective material states

Vgen*: free robot velocity from Step 1. Vgen: temporary robot velocity during the solve. jᵢ: accumulated material-on-body impulse at contact i (N·s).

mᵢ: particle mass. κ: dimensionless effective-mass multiplier. mᵢeff: assumed material mass responding locally at contact i.

Vgen = Vgen*  ;   jᵢ = 0  ;   mᵢeff = κmᵢ
All robot contacts share Vgen. An impulse at one foot can change another foot’s velocity through the robot mass matrix. The box estimator defaults to κ = 10.

B. Compute relative velocity after the accumulated impulses

vaux,i: auxiliary material velocity after impulse −jᵢ. wᵢ: rigid-body surface velocity minus auxiliary material velocity. M and Jᵢ are defined in Steps 1–2.

vaux,i = v̂surface,i − jᵢ / mᵢeff
Vgen = Vgen* + M⁻¹ Σⱼ Jⱼᵀ jⱼ
wᵢ = Vsurface,i − vaux,i = JᵢVgen − vaux,i

For stacked contacts, J stacks point Jacobians; j, w, v̂surface stack their contact vectors. D has diagonal blocks I₃/mᵢeff; I₃ is the 3 × 3 identity.

w = JVgen* − v̂surface + (JM⁻¹Jᵀ + D)j
Effective material mass is retained through D. Auxiliary velocities are not written back to GNN particles.

C. Update normal and tangential contact impulses

nᵢ: unit normal, material → body. dᵢ: signed particle-to-body gap (negative inside). β: penetration correction factor (0.01). γᵢ: gap correction velocity.

Kᵢᵢ: relative-velocity change per impulse at contact i. Kᵢₙₙ: normal component. wᵢₙ, jᵢₙ: normal relative velocity and impulse. I₃ is the 3 × 3 identity.

Kᵢᵢ = JᵢM⁻¹Jᵢᵀ + I₃/mᵢeff  ;   Kᵢₙₙ = nᵢᵀKᵢᵢnᵢ
γᵢ = [max(dᵢ, 0) + β min(dᵢ, 0)] / Δt
jᵢₙcandidate = max(0, jᵢₙ − (wᵢₙ + γᵢ)/Kᵢₙₙ)

μ: contact friction coefficient. jᵢₜ: tangential impulse, constrained by ‖jᵢₜcandidate‖ ≤ μjᵢₙcandidate. α: relaxation (box default 0.1). Δjᵢ: impulse increment.

Δjᵢ = α(jᵢcandidate − jᵢ)  ;   jᵢ ← jᵢ + Δjᵢ

D. Update rigid-object velocity; repeat B → C → D

Δjᵢ: change in accumulated impulse. Kᵢⱼ: velocity response at contact i to an impulse at a different contact j.

Vgen ← Vgen + M⁻¹ Σᵢ Jᵢᵀ Δjᵢ
Kᵢⱼ = JᵢM⁻¹Jⱼᵀ   (i ≠ j)
Articulated formulation: M and J couple contacts across robot links. Current G1 implementation: independent virtual-foot responses approximate this coupling.

Stop when the impulse-correction residual meets tolerance or reaches the iteration limit. Transfer the resulting load to MJWarp, not these temporary velocities.

E. Recover the free-box equations

VCOM, ω: box COM linear velocity and angular velocity. mbox, Iworld: box mass and world inertia about its COM. rᵢ: COM → contact arm. [rᵢ]×: matrix representing cross product with rᵢ.

Vgen = [VCOM; ω]  ;   M = diag(mboxI₃, Iworld)
Jᵢ = [I₃, −[rᵢ]×]
Kᵢₙₙ = 1/mbox + (rᵢ × nᵢ) · Iworld⁻¹(rᵢ × nᵢ) + 1/mᵢeff

ΔVCOM, Δω: velocity increments caused by this iteration’s impulse changes.

ΔVCOM = Σᵢ Δjᵢ/mbox  ;   Δω = Iworld⁻¹Σᵢ(rᵢ × Δjᵢ)
The effective material-mass term is retained. Removing it belongs to the separate fixed-material-velocity experiment.

4. Convert contact impulses to rigid-body loads

τcontactGeneralized material force on the robot’s base and joint degrees of freedom.Flink; TlinkMaterial force on one rigid link; material torque about that link’s center of mass, both in world coordinates.rᵢ; ΔtLink COM → contact arm; simulation timestep. jᵢ is the solved material-on-body impulse.
τcontact = Σᵢ Jᵢᵀjᵢ / Δt
Flink = Σi on link jᵢ / Δt  ;   Tlink = Σi on link (rᵢ × jᵢ) / Δt
Supply either generalized force or per-link force and torque. They describe the same load; do not apply both.

The box runner passes force and COM torque to MJWarp, which advances the actual rigid-body state.

Inputs and outputs · select a step to see its equations
Vgen* = Vgenⁿ + Δt M⁻¹(τknown − b); Vgen = Vgen* + M⁻¹ΣJᵢᵀjᵢ; vaux,i = v̂surface,i − jᵢ/(κmᵢ); w = JVgen* − v̂surface + (JM⁻¹Jᵀ + D)j, D = blockdiag(I₃/(κmᵢ)). Generalized material force: τcontact = ΣJᵢᵀjᵢ/Δt. A free box has Vgen = [VCOM; ω] and Jᵢ = [I₃, −[rᵢ]×].
Full articulated extension with effective material mass. Generalized dynamics reference · Separate fixed-velocity experiment.
This derives the articulated extension of the effective-material-mass contact model; it is not a claim that the current estimator implements full robot coupling. q, M and contact Jacobians are frozen during each solve. M⁻¹ denotes a linear solve, not an explicitly formed inverse. The free-velocity equation illustrates explicit integration. G_i is defined as velocity-component derivatives with respect to position. The Warp fit_gradients kernel stores its transpose, and prepare_contacts transposes the stored matrix before extrapolation. The GNN predicts endpoint velocity separately from displacement; it is not generally equal to predicted displacement divided by timestep. An implementation must use a response consistent with its integrator, including implicit damping or actuation if present. Active joint limits, loop constraints and rigid-ground contacts require additional constraint equations or a constraint-consistent response; an unconstrained M⁻¹ alone does not account for them. The current G1 estimator instead uses independent virtual-foot velocities and inertias prepared by the coupler. Actuation and gravity are already included in those prepared velocities. The finite material response D is an assumed local, independent-contact approximation, not a learned or measured constitutive law. It is retained in this derivation and in the default box implementation.
10 / Contact Force Estimation: Effective Mass

Effective mass supplies the missing contact response

jᵢ: material-on-body impulse. mᵢ: particle mass. κ: effective-mass multiplier. Δvaux,i: assumed local material-velocity change in the contact solve.

Impulse on body: +jᵢ   ·   impulse on material: −jᵢ
Assumed local velocity change: Δvaux,i = −jᵢ / (κ mᵢ)

What this equation supplies

A relation between the unknown impulse and the material’s response. Together with body inertia and contact constraints, it determines the iterative contact update.

What κ means

κ scales particle mass in this response. Larger κ gives a smaller local velocity change for the same impulse. It approximates resistance from surrounding material.

This is an auxiliary contact model, not a second particle update. The GNN already predicts particle motion including interaction; applying −jᵢ again to its stored velocity would count contact twice.

κ is an estimator parameter, not a measured material law. Current defaults differ: κ = 10 for the unified box runner; κ = 100 for the published G1 filtering benchmark.

13 / Implementation: Scope and Assumptions

Keep model assumptions explicit

ComponentCurrent scope
GNN geometryOne fixed-size box and a ground plane; analytic distance queries. General meshes are not encoded.
Particle predictionMotion history and material features; no explicit stress or plastic-state evolution.
Optional activity selectionA learned mask can keep inactive particles fixed and set their velocity to zero. It is enabled only when a selector is supplied; the base GNN still evaluates the full graph.
Contact reconstructionEffective-mass approximation and friction constraints; no particle–particle internal-force reconstruction.
Parameter consistencyThe GNN boundary input fixes friction at 0.6. The unified box runner supplies recorded body contact friction (0.6 for older files); the standalone estimator default is 0.48.

Implementation does not establish accuracy: evaluate particle rollout error and coupled body motion separately. Numerical results and videos are kept in the linked experiment reports.
