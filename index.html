aᵢ and bᵢ are separate three-component network outputs in m/s; aᵢ is not acceleration. The decoder scales both by 0.2 m/s. Δt = 0.02 s.

What is learned

Gravity, material interaction, and boundary response are included in the learned corrections. There is no separate gravity integration or geometric projection in the current particle update.

The activity mask freezes inactive particles after this update. Endpoint velocity is supervised separately from displacement.
07 / Particle GNN: Region Selection Enabled by --selector

Predict which particles should move

Input: current features only

Particle velocity history and material parameters; local box/ground features; neighboring particle speeds. The selector is an MLP, not another GNN.

Apply the mask to the GNN output

Active: keep predicted next position and velocity.
Inactive: keep current position; set next velocity to zero.

Train the selector separately

A reference particle is active if it moves more than 0.5 mm from its current position within the next 0.1 s. Future MPM positions supply training labels only; inference uses current features.

Current limit: all particles still enter GNN message passing. The mask reduces unwanted motion; it does not yet reduce graph computation.

Results: region selection reduces stationary-particle drift. Those tests replay recorded box motion; they do not establish accurate coupled box support. ScoopBot instead predicts a spatial RoI and runs dynamics on the active subgraph.
48 input values: 26 particle-node values, 18 masked collider values, two collider influence values, and two neighboring-speed values. SiLU hidden layers. The threshold is stored in the selector checkpoint. No future particle or box state enters inference.

09 / Contact Force Estimation: Method

From particle velocity to body force

Inputs → body force and torque

PARTICLES

Current positions xᵢⁿ, masses mᵢ, and next velocities v̂ᵢⁿ⁺¹ supplied by the GNN.

RIGID BODY

Current pose, linear/angular velocity, mass, inertia and geometry; known external force and torque.

OUTPUT

Material force F and torque τ on the body, about its center of mass.

Response assumption: finite effective mass (baseline), or fixed supplied material velocity (experiment: Step 3, panel E). Momentum conservation alone does not determine the contact response.

Geometry stays fixed during the solve. Auxiliary contact velocities are not written back to the GNN particles.

1. Compute free rigid-body velocity

Predict velocity under gravity and known external loads, before adding the material reaction.

V* = Vⁿ + Δt (g + Fext / m)
ω* = ωⁿ + Δt I⁻¹ [τext − ωⁿ × (Iωⁿ)]

I is body inertia about its center of mass, in world coordinates. Fext and τext exclude the material reaction; gravity is included separately.

Step 3 initializes V = V* and ω = ω*, then updates both using contact impulses. MJWarp subsequently integrates the actual body state.

2. Estimate velocity at the contact point

For each material particle, find its nearest point sᵢ on the box. Fit a local velocity gradient Gᵢ from neighboring predicted particle velocities.

ûᵢ = v̂ᵢⁿ⁺¹ + Gᵢ (sᵢ − xᵢⁿ)
rᵢ = sᵢ − cⁿ

cⁿ is the body center of mass; rᵢ is the contact arm. Surface points are derived from particle queries, not sampled independently.

Keep particles already touching or able to reach the surface during Δt. Use the surface gap and normal to constrain each contact.

3. Solve individual contact impulses

A. Initialize the effective-mass baseline

V = V*   ;   ω = ω*   ;   jᵢ = 0
mᵢeff = κ mᵢ

V*, ω*: Step 1’s box velocities after gravity and known external loads, before material contact.

V, ω: temporary box velocities updated during this solve. jᵢ: accumulated material-on-box impulse at contact i. κ: effective material-mass multiplier.

Every contact uses the same V and ω. The box pose and contact geometry stay fixed during the iterations.

B. Relative velocity with effective material mass

vᵢbox = V + ω × rᵢ
uᵢ = ûᵢ − jᵢ / mᵢeff
wᵢ = vᵢbox − uᵢ = V + ω × rᵢ − ûᵢ + jᵢ / mᵢeff

rᵢ: box COM → contact point. ûᵢ: material velocity extrapolated to that point in Step 2. uᵢ: auxiliary material velocity after the opposite impulse −jᵢ. wᵢ: relative velocity.

Initially, V = V* and ω = ω*. Later iterations use the velocities modified by all contact impulses.

C. Correct normal approach and tangential slip

jᵢₙcandidate = max(0, jᵢₙ − (wᵢₙ + bᵢ) / Kᵢₙₙ)
Kᵢₙₙ = 1/M + (rᵢ × nᵢ) · I⁻¹(rᵢ × nᵢ) + 1/mᵢeff
bᵢ = [max(dᵢ, 0) + β min(dᵢ, 0)] / Δt

nᵢ: material → box normal; wᵢₙ = nᵢ · wᵢ. M, I: box mass and world inertia. dᵢ: signed particle-to-box gap (negative inside). β = 0.01.

Kᵢₙₙ includes box translation, box rotation and effective material response. Then solve tangential friction with ‖jᵢₜcandidate‖ ≤ μ jᵢₙcandidate.

Δjᵢ = 0.1 (jᵢcandidate − jᵢ)   ;   jᵢ ← jᵢ + Δjᵢ

D. Update box velocities; repeat B → C → D

V ← V + Σᵢ Δjᵢ / M
ω ← ω + I⁻¹ Σᵢ (rᵢ × Δjᵢ)

Δjᵢ is the change in accumulated impulse, expressed in world coordinates. All contacts are evaluated in parallel, then their changes are combined.

V = V* + Σᵢ jᵢ / M   ;   ω = ω* + I⁻¹ Σᵢ (rᵢ × jᵢ)

Stop when the impulse-correction residual meets tolerance, or at the iteration limit. Step 4 converts the accumulated impulses to force and torque.

Only the wrench goes to MJWarp. Temporary box velocities and auxiliary material velocities are not copied into the simulated states.

E. Experiment: hold supplied material velocity fixed

Baseline: uᵢ = ûᵢ − jᵢ / (κmᵢ)   →   Fixed variant: uᵢ = ûᵢ
wᵢ = V + ω × rᵢ − ûᵢ
Kᵢₙₙ = 1/M + (rᵢ × nᵢ) · I⁻¹(rᵢ × nᵢ)

Remove the material inverse-mass term from both relative velocity and contact response. No mass multiplier is used. V and ω still start at V*, ω* and change with the contact impulses (D).

Same gap correction and friction limit. Parallel update fraction: 1 / max(Ncontacts, 10), instead of the baseline’s 0.1, to limit simultaneous corrections.

Measured outcome: better stationary support, worse dynamic motion; frequent unconverged solves. Some imposed contact velocities are incompatible with one rigid body. Results, force profiles and videos →

4. Sum impulses into a body wrench

F = Σᵢ jᵢ / Δt
τ = Σᵢ (rᵢ × jᵢ) / Δt

F is material force on the body. τ is material torque about its center of mass. Both are expressed in world coordinates.

This is the final conversion from solved contact impulses to force and torque. It is not a reconstruction from total particle momentum alone.

The box runner applies this wrench during the upcoming MJWarp substeps. It does not copy the estimator’s temporary body velocity into the simulator.

Inputs and outputs · select a step to see its equations
V = V* + Σjᵢ/M; ω = ω* + I⁻¹Σ(rᵢ × jᵢ). Material: uᵢ = ûᵢ − jᵢ/(κmᵢ). Relative velocity: wᵢ = V + ω × rᵢ − uᵢ. Solve compressive, friction-limited impulses; F = Σjᵢ/Δt and τ = Σ(rᵢ × jᵢ)/Δt.
The estimator reads particle motion and estimates body reactions. Neither response mode integrates the particles. Fixed-velocity experiment.
10 / Contact Force Estimation: Effective Mass

Effective mass supplies the missing contact response

Impulse on body: +jᵢ   ·   impulse on material: −jᵢ
Assumed local velocity change: Δuᵢ = −jᵢ / (κ mᵢ)

What this equation supplies

A relation between the unknown impulse and the material’s response. Together with body inertia and contact constraints, it determines the iterative contact update.

What κ means

κ scales particle mass in this response. Larger κ gives a smaller local velocity change for the same impulse. It approximates resistance from surrounding material.

This is an auxiliary contact model, not a second particle update. The GNN already predicts particle motion including interaction; applying −jᵢ again to its stored velocity would count contact twice.

κ is an estimator parameter, not a measured material law. Current defaults differ: κ = 10 for the unified box runner; κ = 100 for the published G1 filtering benchmark.

13 / Implementation: Scope and Assumptions

Keep model assumptions explicit

ComponentCurrent scope
GNN geometryOne fixed-size box and a ground plane; analytic distance queries. General meshes are not encoded.
Particle predictionMotion history and material features; no explicit stress or plastic-state evolution.
Optional activity selectionA learned mask can keep inactive particles fixed and set their velocity to zero. It is enabled only when a selector is supplied; the base GNN still evaluates the full graph.
Contact reconstructionEffective-mass approximation and friction constraints; no particle–particle internal-force reconstruction.
Parameter consistencyThe GNN boundary input fixes friction at 0.6. The unified box runner supplies recorded body contact friction (0.6 for older files); the standalone estimator default is 0.48.

Implementation does not establish accuracy: evaluate particle rollout error and coupled body motion separately. Numerical results and videos are kept in the linked experiment reports.
