03 / Diagnosis: Why Did \(F_z\) Diverge in Fig 1 & Fig 3 While Fig 2 Looked Good?

Root cause isolated (Variant A vs. Variant C comparison in the updated plots below):
Comparing the dash-dot blue curve (`PhysicsContactHead (A: MPM particles)`) against the solid teal curve (`PhysicsContactHead (C: rollout particles)`) directly answers whether the discrepancy comes from the neural force head or from open-loop particle rollout mismatch:
  1. Why Fig 3 (vertical-drop) dropped to ~35 N after impact under rollout particles (teal), but stays at 66.6 N (vs. 73.6 N ref) under MPM particles (blue dash-dot):
    • In the vertical-drop test, the 7.5 kg box is released from \(z = 0.75\text{ m}\) in mid-air and slams into the sand bed at \(t = 0.30\text{ s}\) at \(-3.0\text{ m/s}\). Note that the 776 training episodes only started recording training windows after settling or mild settling; high-speed free-fall cratering was an unseen out-of-distribution test for the particle network.
    • When we inspect the particle positions around the box at \(t = 4.0\text{ s}\) (step 200) in vertical-drop:
      • In MPM reference (`Variant A`), only 5 particles lie above the box top face (\(n_z > 0.5\)), while 165 particles sit compressed beneath the box bottom face (\(n_z < -0.5\), contact weight \(\sum \phi_{\text{under}} = 58.9\)). Fed these true MPM particle positions, PhysicsContactHead predicts 66.6 N upward support (within 9% of the 73.6 N box weight) and a 520.9 N impact peak!
      • In Transolver open-loop particle rollout (`Variant C`), during the violent impact at \(t = 0.35\text{ s}\), the particle model fails to clear all sand out of the box's volume: 52 predicted particles end up trapped above/inside the upper half of the box (\(n_z > +0.5\), mean penetration gap \(-2.30\text{ cm}\), \(\sum \phi_{\text{above}} = 55.3\)), while the particles beneath the box thin out (\(\sum \phi_{\text{under}} = 28.5\)). Because our contact head is strictly physical (\(\mathbf{f}_{n,i} = -f_{n,i}\mathbf{n}_i\)), particles with outward normal \(\mathbf{n}_i = +\hat{\mathbf{z}}\) (top face of the box) push downward (\(-32\text{ N}\)) on the box, subtracting from the upward bottom-face support!
  2. Why Fig 1 (wide-circle) vs. Fig 2 (figure-eight) behaved differently in the second half (\(t = 2.5\text{–}5.0\text{ s}\)):
    • In open-loop recorded-box evaluation (`Variant C`), the box trajectory is replayed from the MPM recording while particles evolve autoregressively. Even a 1.5 cm vertical discrepancy between the predicted sand surface height and the recorded MPM box height changes the apparent penetration depth \(\text{ReLU}(-g_{\text{eff}})\):
      • In wide-circle (Fig 1), the box sweeps a continuous outer perimeter where predicted particles pile up ~1–2 cm higher along the circular berm than in MPM (at \(t = 4.0\text{ s}\), 96 rollout particles vs. 40 MPM particles are within 8 cm of the box). Because the recorded MPM box is forced through that higher predicted pile without being allowed to rise in \(z\), the contact head sees deeper penetration and outputs higher upward \(F_z\) (65–95 N vs. 50–75 N).
      • In figure-eight (Fig 2), the box crosses back through the center furrow it just excavated, so the local particle density around the box stays balanced (60 rollout particles vs. 28 MPM particles) and \(F_z\) tracks the 55–70 N reference closely.
      • Crucial proof from Closed-Loop Simulation (Section 02 above): When the box is actually allowed to move dynamically in response to `PhysicsContactHead` forces (`closed-loop-box.png`), the box naturally adjusts its riding height by a few millimeters to reach vertical force equilibrium, and the closed-loop box position error is only 4.39 cm (wide-circle) and 5.01 cm (figure-eight)!
